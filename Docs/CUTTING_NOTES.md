# Cutting panels open

For the README (three sentences):

Sealed panels are now held by seams that you cut along like real metal: hold the plasma cutter's flame a few centimetres off the orange seam and drag it steadily, and a white-hot line follows the flame and cools through orange and dull red to a dark kerf while sparks and molten droplets spray off the cut. Pace matters: a steady pass at a careful drawing speed cuts clean and keeps the cutter cool, too fast only scores the metal (a thinner glow you must go over again), and holding still just burns heat, and the cutter tells you through the buzz in your hand, the pitch of the flame and its screen ("SEAM 5/12 GOOD", "TOO FAST", "MOVE ON", "CLOSER"). The plate works loose on the side you have cut and pops free with glowing edges when the last stretch goes, and a crew mate's cut shows on your headset as the same glowing line moving along the seam.

## How it works (for developers)

- A panel's seam is its `Cuttable.cutPoints`: each `CutPoint` is now a straight stretch (`length`, local X along the seam, local Z out of the face) of 16 cells with a depth and a temperature. The network still sees one weld per stretch, so PointCutReport / PointCut / CutProgress / the weld bitset are unchanged (no wire changes).
- The Builder's `MakeCuttable` keeps its signature and placement; its weld anchors now define the seam: each plate side with two or more anchors near it is welded between them, a lone anchor is a clamp cut across the plate, and runs are split into stretches of at most 0.34 m. Locker and reactor housings: 12 stretches (3.92 m all round); service hatches and ducts: 6 (both sides, 1.8 m); rack clamps: 2; the tug's practice plate: 8.
- `SeamCut` holds the cutting maths. The flame meets the metal along the nozzle's forward: full strength up to 5 cm away (none past 9 cm), roughly facing the face (none beyond about 78 degrees), within 2.5 cm of the line (none past 5 cm). A cell needs 0.12 s under the middle of the 5 cm spot, so a steady pass at up to 0.40 m/s cuts clean in one go; faster it bites less (at 0.6 m/s about half depth). A stretch is through when its mean depth reaches 0.92 and no cell is under 0.75.
- Heat: firing in the air or dwelling heats as before (7 s to overheat, 5 s to cool); while the flame bites cleanly into fresh metal the cutter heats up to 2.5 times more slowly. A careful steady cut of a locker panel at 0.36 m/s takes about 11 s and ends at about 68 % heat; the old six-weld panel took about 10 to 12 s for a careful player. A skilled player at 0.40 m/s takes about 10 s.
- A co-op client's weld reports from the flame are paced at 4 at once and 3 a second (the host allows 6 and 4 per player, shared with door requests); a clean cut completes about 1.2 stretches a second. CutProgress also goes out once more when the flame leaves a stretch unfinished. A crew mate's progress is drawn in along the stretch over the message interval, glowing as it goes.
- `CutSeamVisual` draws the bead, kerf and molten line for a whole panel as one small vertex-coloured mesh on the plate (one draw call, rebuilt only while something changes). The cutter's sparks and droplets are emitted at the contact point; its one flame light flickers warm while cutting. The cutter's model, materials and place in the hand are unchanged.
- Tests: `CuttingSeamPlayTests` and `CuttingSeamNetPlayTests` drive the real cutter through `PlasmaCutterTool.ManualDrive` / `Step`. Previews: `80_seam_cut_closeup`, `80b_seam_cut_closeup_behind`, `81_seam_cut_one_metre`, `82_seam_cut_corner_kerf`, `83_cutter_screen_seam` (`PreviewRenderer.RenderCutPreviews` renders only these).
